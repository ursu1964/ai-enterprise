---

# 1. Purpose

R4 defines the first AI-enabled capability of AI-Enterprise.

Its purpose is to convert unstructured client input into candidate structured project knowledge while preserving provenance, uncertainty, human control, and deterministic validation.

R4 does not permit AI to become an authoritative source of business truth.

The AI interpretation layer shall:

* read client-provided source material
* identify candidate project concepts
* map those concepts into AEPM and AEIR structures
* identify ambiguity and missing information
* propose clarification questions
* detect probable contradictions
* record complete AI-operation provenance
* submit all candidate knowledge for deterministic validation and human review

R4 builds on the R3 foundation and shall not weaken any R3 control.

---

# 2. Implementation Objective

R4 shall support the following workflow:

```text
Client Source Material
    ↓
Source Registration
    ↓
Content Normalization
    ↓
AI Interpretation Request
    ↓
Structured Candidate Extraction
    ↓
Schema Validation
    ↓
Candidate AEPM and AEIR Objects
    ↓
Deterministic Validation
    ↓
Ambiguity and Gap Analysis
    ↓
Clarification Questions
    ↓
Human Review
    ↓
Approve, Reject, or Edit
    ↓
Canonical Object Version
    ↓
Project Snapshot
```

At the completion of R4, AI-Enterprise shall be capable of transforming client prose into reviewable, traceable candidate project knowledge.

---

# 3. R4 Success Condition

R4 is complete when the system can:

1. Accept unstructured client text as a registered source.
2. Normalize the text into machine-processable source segments.
3. invoke one configured AI model using a versioned interpretation contract.
4. receive structured output conforming to an approved response schema.
5. reject malformed or schema-invalid AI output.
6. create candidate AEPM and AEIR objects from valid AI output.
7. mark all AI-derived knowledge with appropriate truth and approval status.
8. preserve traceability to exact source segments.
9. record model, prompt, schema, parameters, and execution provenance.
10. generate structured ambiguity and missing-information findings.
11. produce candidate clarification questions.
12. allow a human reviewer to approve, reject, edit, or defer each candidate object.
13. promote only approved candidate knowledge into the canonical project model.
14. prevent AI from directly modifying approved knowledge.
15. reproduce the interpretation operation from its saved inputs and configuration, subject to model availability.
16. pass automated evaluation against representative source examples.

# 5. R4 Architectural Principles

## 5.1 AI Is an Interpreter, Not an Authority

The AI layer may interpret, classify, infer, and propose.

It shall not silently establish approved business knowledge.

## 5.2 Structured Output Is Mandatory

Free-form AI responses shall not be accepted as canonical input.

Every interpretation operation shall return output conforming to a formal JSON Schema.

## 5.3 Deterministic Validation Follows AI Interpretation

All AI output shall pass:

1. response-schema validation
2. identifier validation
3. object-model validation
4. relationship validation
5. deterministic project validation

## 5.4 Provenance Is Mandatory

Every AI-derived object, relationship, assumption, contradiction, or question shall reference:

* the AI operation
* the source
* the exact source segment
* the prompt version
* the model
* the output schema version

## 5.5 Human Approval Is Required

AI-derived knowledge shall default to:

```yaml
truth_status: inferred
approval_status: pending
```

No AI-derived candidate may enter an approved snapshot before explicit human review.

## 5.6 Approved Knowledge Is Protected

AI interpretation operations shall not update approved objects directly.

Changes to approved knowledge shall be represented as proposed revisions.

## 5.7 Unknowns Must Remain Unknown

When the source does not support a conclusion, the AI shall return:

* unknown
* ambiguous
* unsupported
* assumption_required
* clarification_required

The AI shall not fill gaps with plausible but unsupported content.

# 7. Source Intake Model

## 7.1 Supported Source Types

R4 shall initially support:

* plain text
* Markdown
* manually pasted client manifesto
* UTF-8 text files
* structured notes exported as text

R4 may accept JSON or YAML as raw text, but machine-readable AEPM files should continue through the deterministic R3 import path.

## 7.2 Source Registration

Every source shall be registered before AI interpretation.

```yaml
source:
  id: "SRC-002"
  project_id: "PRJ-001"
  source_type: "client_manifest_text"
  name: "Initial Client Vision"
  media_type: "text/plain"
  language: "en"
  storage_location: "sources/PRJ-001/SRC-002.txt"
  checksum: "sha256:..."
  captured_at: "2026-08-04T19:30:00+02:00"
  captured_by: "USR-001"
  processing_status: "registered"
```

## 7.3 Source Processing Status

Allowed values:

* registered
* normalization_pending
* normalized
* interpretation_pending
* interpreted
* failed
* archived

# 8. Source Normalization

## 8.1 Purpose

Source normalization prepares unstructured text for deterministic segmentation and AI interpretation.

Normalization shall not alter business meaning.

## 8.2 Normalization Operations

R4 may perform:

* Unicode normalization
* line-ending normalization
* removal of invalid control characters
* whitespace normalization
* preservation of paragraph boundaries
* heading detection
* list-boundary detection
* source-language detection
* stable segment numbering

R4 shall not:

* rewrite client text
* summarize the source before storage
* silently correct terminology
* remove apparently duplicated statements
* resolve contradictions
* translate content without explicit instruction

## 8.3 Normalized Source Structure

```yaml
normalized_source:
  source_id: "SRC-002"
  normalization_version: "0.1"
  language: "en"
  character_count: 4180
  segment_count: 18
  normalized_at: "2026-08-04T19:31:00+02:00"
  checksum: "sha256:..."
```

## 9.3 Segment Types

* heading
* paragraph
* list_item
* table_row
* quoted_statement
* metadata
* unknown

## 9.4 Segment Stability

Segment identifiers shall remain stable for the same normalized source version.

If source content changes, a new source version shall be created.

# 11. Prompt Registry

## 11.1 Purpose

All AI instructions shall be versioned and auditable.

No production interpretation operation may use an unregistered prompt.

## 11.2 Prompt Definition

```yaml
prompt:
  id: "PROMPT-AEPM-EXTRACTOR"
  version: "0.1.0"
  name: "AEPM Candidate Extractor"
  operation_type: "manifest_extraction"
  system_instruction_ref: "instructions/system-aepm-extractor-0.1.0.md"
  task_template_ref: "instructions/task-aepm-extractor-0.1.0.md"
  response_schema_ref: "schemas/AI-EXTRACTION-RESPONSE-0.1.schema.json"
  status: "active"
  created_at: "2026-08-04T19:00:00+02:00"
  approved_by: "USR-ARCH-001"
```

## 11.3 Prompt Status

* draft
* evaluation
* active
* deprecated
* retired

## 11.4 Prompt Requirements

The extraction prompt shall instruct the AI to:

* use only the supplied source material
* distinguish direct assertions from inference
* avoid inventing missing business facts
* cite source segment IDs
* return unknown where evidence is insufficient
* mark assumptions explicitly
* produce only schema-conforming structured output
* avoid technology recommendations unless present in the source
* preserve client terminology
* avoid approval decisions
* generate clarification needs separately from extracted facts

# 15. Evidence and Support Classification

## 15.1 Support Types

Every candidate shall classify its source support as one of:

* direct
* contextual
* inferred
* assumed
* unsupported

## 15.2 Mapping to Truth Status

Recommended initial mapping:

```text
direct       → asserted
contextual   → asserted or inferred
inferred     → inferred
assumed      → assumed
unsupported  → disputed or rejected candidate
```

This mapping shall be configurable but deterministic.

## 15.3 Unsupported Candidates

A candidate with only unsupported evidence shall not be promoted.

The system may retain it as:

* a rejected candidate
* a clarification topic
* a recommendation explicitly separated from client truth

# 17. Candidate Identifier Handling

## 17.1 Proposed Identifiers

The AI may propose identifiers, but the platform shall validate or replace them.

## 17.2 Canonical Identifier Assignment

The canonical identifier service shall:

* enforce object-type prefixes
* guarantee project-level uniqueness
* avoid reuse of retired IDs
* preserve stable IDs after promotion
* record proposed and assigned identifiers

Example:

```yaml
identifier_assignment:
  candidate_id: "CAND-OBJ-0001"
  proposed_id: "INT-001"
  assigned_id: "INT-004"
  assignment_reason: "INT-001 already exists"
```

AI shall never be the final authority for canonical identifiers.

# 18. Candidate Deduplication

## 18.1 Deterministic Duplicate Checks

Before human review, the system shall detect:

* duplicate proposed IDs
* exact duplicate names
* identical normalized descriptions
* exact duplicate relationship triples
* candidates matching an existing canonical source pointer

## 18.2 Semantic Duplicate Suggestions

AI may suggest semantic duplicates, but these shall remain non-binding.

```yaml
duplicate_suggestion:
  candidate_id: "CAND-OBJ-0012"
  existing_object_id: "CAP-003"
  similarity_type: "probable_semantic_duplicate"
  confidence: 0.86
  rationale: "Both describe stock reservation."
  status: "pending_review"
```

# 20. Assumption Model

## 20.1 Assumption Structure

```yaml
assumption:
  id: "ASM-001"
  project_id: "PRJ-001"
  ai_operation_id: "AIOP-0001"

  statement: "The platform will support multiple warehouses."

  reason:
    "The source refers to warehouse operations in plural but does not explicitly define scope."

  source_refs:
    - source_id: "SRC-002"
      segment_id: "SEG-002-0004"

  affected_candidate_refs:
    - "CAND-OBJ-0006"

  confidence: 0.62
  approval_status: "pending"
  status: "open"
```

## 20.2 Assumption Rules

* assumptions shall never use `truth_status: verified`
* assumptions shall require review
* assumptions may become asserted or verified only through supporting evidence or human approval
* rejected assumptions shall not remain active in canonical knowledge
* material assumptions may block snapshot approval

# 21. Probable Contradiction Model

## 21.1 Purpose

AI may identify probable contradictions that require deterministic comparison or human review.

## 21.2 Contradiction Structure

```yaml
probable_contradiction:
  id: "PCON-001"
  project_id: "PRJ-001"
  ai_operation_id: "AIOP-0001"

  statement_a:
    source_id: "SRC-002"
    segment_id: "SEG-002-0008"
    text: "All warehouse users must authenticate."

  statement_b:
    source_id: "SRC-002"
    segment_id: "SEG-002-0015"
    text: "Temporary workers should access receiving without accounts."

  contradiction_type: "security_access_conflict"

  confidence: 0.91
  severity: "error"
  blocking_recommendation: true

  explanation:
    "The two statements define incompatible access requirements unless an alternative temporary identity mechanism exists."

  status: "pending_review"
```

## 21.3 Contradiction Limitation

The AI shall label these as probable contradictions.

Only deterministic rules or human review may establish a confirmed contradiction.

# 23. Clarification Question Engine

## 23.1 Purpose

The question engine converts findings, ambiguities, assumptions, and probable contradictions into structured client questions.

## 23.2 Question Structure

```yaml
clarification_question:
  id: "QUE-001"
  project_id: "PRJ-001"

  origin_type: "ambiguity"
  origin_ref: "AMB-001"

  category: "quality_requirement"
  priority: "high"
  blocking: false

  question:
    "What maximum delay is acceptable for inventory updates?"

  explanation:
    "The phrase 'real-time inventory' is not measurable."

  expected_answer_type: "duration"

  answer_options:
    - "Less than 1 second"

    - "Less than 30 seconds"
    - "Other"

  affected_candidate_refs:
    - "CAND-OBJ-0009"

  source_refs:
    - source_id: "SRC-002"
      segment_id: "SEG-002-0011"

  status: "open"
```

## 23.3 Expected Answer Types

* free_text
* boolean
* integer
* decimal
* percentage
* currency
* duration
* date
* datetime
* single_choice
* multiple_choice
* stakeholder_reference
* object_reference
* structured_metric

## 23.4 Question Quality Rules

A clarification question shall:

* ask one primary question
* identify why the answer is needed
* connect to affected objects
* avoid embedding unsupported assumptions
* preserve client terminology
* avoid technical implementation detail unless relevant
* avoid combining unrelated uncertainties
* include a structured expected answer type

## 23.5 Question Priorities

* critical
* high
* medium
* low

# 24. Human Review Workflow

## 24.1 Review Actions

For each candidate object or relationship, the reviewer may:

* approve
* approve with edits
* reject
* defer
* merge with existing object
* request clarification
* classify as recommendation
* classify as unsupported

## 24.2 Review Record

```yaml
candidate_review:
  id: "REV-001"
  candidate_id: "CAND-OBJ-0001"
  reviewer_id: "USR-002"

  action: "approve_with_edits"

  rationale: "Clarified terminology used by the client."

  reviewed_at: "2026-08-04T20:15:00+02:00"
```

## 24.3 Review Authority

The platform shall distinguish:

* editor
* business reviewer
* technical reviewer
* project approver
* administrator

A reviewer shall only perform actions permitted by role and project authority.

## 24.4 Bulk Review

R4 may support bulk approval only when:

* all selected candidates passed schema validation
* no selected candidate has a blocking finding
* all candidates have direct source support
* no candidate modifies an approved canonical object
* the reviewer has appropriate authority

# 25. Promotion to Canonical AEIR

## 25.1 Promotion Preconditions

A candidate may be promoted when:

* response schema validation passed
* AEIR schema validation passed
* deterministic validation completed
* reviewer approval exists
* assigned canonical identifier is valid
* source traceability is complete
* any required clarification has been resolved

## 25.2 Promotion Operation

Promotion shall:

1. create a canonical object or relationship version
2. preserve candidate provenance
3. create source links
4. create AI-operation links
5. create an event-log entry
6. update candidate status to promoted
7. preserve the original candidate payload
8. record reviewer edits
9. rerun project validation

## 25.3 Promoted Object Example

```yaml
id: "INT-004"
project_id: "PRJ-001"
type: "Intent"
name: "Reduce inventory discrepancies"
description: "Reduce differences between physical and recorded stock."

lifecycle_status: "draft"
truth_status: "asserted"
approval_status: "approved"

confidence: 0.97
version: 1

source_refs:
  - "SRC-002"

evidence_refs:
  - "AIOP-0001"
  - "REV-001"

metadata:
  origin_candidate_id: "CAND-OBJ-0001"
  ai_derived: true
```


## 27.3 Raw Responses

Raw provider responses may be stored for audit purposes, subject to:

* security policy
* data-retention policy
* privacy requirements
* provider terms
* encryption requirements

Canonical processing shall rely on validated structured output, not raw provider text.

# 28. Retry and Failure Handling

## 28.1 Retry Conditions

The orchestrator may retry when:

* transient provider error occurs
* rate limiting occurs
* response is truncated
* schema validation fails due to recoverable formatting
* operation timeout occurs

## 28.2 Retry Limits

Default maximum:

* two automatic retries for transient provider errors
* one schema-repair attempt
* no automatic retry after policy refusal
* no automatic retry after explicit unsupported-language result

## 28.3 Schema Repair

A schema-repair operation may receive:

* invalid structured output
* schema validation errors
* the same response schema

It shall not receive additional business knowledge or silently reinterpret the source.

## 28.4 Failure Record

```yaml
ai_operation_failure:
  operation_id: "AIOP-0002"
  failure_type: "schema_validation"
  retry_count: 1
  final_status: "schema_failed"
  error_summary: "candidate_objects[2].source_support is missing"
  occurred_at: "2026-08-04T20:00:00+02:00"
```

---

# 29. Security Requirements

## 29.1 Data Isolation

Project source material shall not be mixed across tenants or projects.

## 29.2 Prompt Injection Controls

The source text shall be treated as untrusted content.

The interpretation system shall:

* clearly separate system instructions from source text
* mark source text as data
* prohibit source text from redefining system rules
* prohibit source instructions from changing output schemas
* reject requests to reveal system prompts
* avoid executing commands embedded in source content
* record detected prompt-injection indicators

## 29.3 Secret Handling

The system shall not send secrets to the AI model unless explicitly required and authorized.

Secrets detected in source material should be:

* redacted
* tokenized
* quarantined
* or excluded from interpretation

according to platform policy.

## 29.4 Sensitive Data

Sources containing sensitive data shall be classified before model submission.

The platform shall support policies such as:

* allowed external model
* private model only
* redaction required
* interpretation prohibited

## 29.5 Provider Configuration

Provider credentials shall be stored in a secure secret-management system.

They shall not appear in:

* prompts
* logs
* candidate payloads
* event records
* API responses

---

# 30. Privacy and Retention

The platform shall record:

* what source data was sent
* which provider received it
* when it was sent
* what retention policy applied
* whether provider-side retention was enabled or disabled
* which user initiated the operation

AI-operation data retention shall be configurable by tenant and project policy.

# 32. AI Evaluation Framework

## 32.1 Purpose

R4 shall not be accepted based solely on qualitative demonstrations.

The extraction layer shall be evaluated against a controlled dataset.

## 32.2 Evaluation Dataset

The initial dataset should contain:

* clear project manifesto
* incomplete manifesto
* contradictory manifesto
* ambiguous manifesto
* technology-heavy manifesto
* business-only manifesto
* manifesto containing unsupported assumptions
* manifesto containing duplicate concepts
* manifesto containing prompt-injection text
* manifesto with mixed approved and speculative statements

## 32.3 Ground-Truth Structure

Each evaluation case shall define:

* expected object types
* expected direct facts
* expected unknowns
* expected ambiguities
* expected assumptions
* expected contradictions
* prohibited invented facts
* expected source-segment links

## 32.4 Core Metrics

R4 shall measure:

### Object Extraction Precision

Correct extracted objects divided by all extracted objects.

### Object Extraction Recall

Correct extracted objects divided by expected objects.

### Source Attribution Accuracy

Candidates linked to correct source segments divided by all candidates.

### Unsupported Invention Rate

Unsupported candidates divided by all candidates.

### Classification Accuracy

Correct object-type classifications divided by evaluated classifications.

### Ambiguity Detection Recall

Detected expected ambiguities divided by total expected ambiguities.

### Schema Compliance Rate

Schema-valid responses divided by all interpretation responses.

### Human Acceptance Rate

Approved or approved-with-edit candidates divided by reviewed candidates.

### Edit Distance to Approval

Amount of human correction required before promotion.

## 32.5 Critical Acceptance Metric

The unsupported invention rate shall be treated as a critical safety and quality metric.

A low recall may produce more questions.

A high invention rate corrupts project truth.

---

# 33. Initial Quality Thresholds

R4 v0.1 should target:

```text
Schema compliance rate:          99% after permitted repair
Source attribution accuracy:     at least 95%
Unsupported invention rate:      below 2%
Object extraction precision:     at least 90%
Object extraction recall:        at least 80%
Human acceptance rate:           at least 80%
Blocking ambiguity recall:       at least 85%
```

These values are initial engineering targets and may be revised through evaluation.

Failure to meet them shall not be hidden by increasing human review workload without analysis.

* mock provider

* extraction request

* candidate object creation
* candidate relationship creation

# 40. R4 Acceptance Criteria

R4 shall be accepted only when all of the following are true:

* Unstructured text can be registered as a source.
* Normalized source segments have stable identifiers.
* AI instructions and prompts are versioned.
* Model access is isolated behind an adapter.
* AI output conforms to formal schemas.
* Invalid output is rejected.
* All candidates remain separate from canonical knowledge.
* Every candidate traces to exact source segments.
* AI-generated knowledge defaults to pending approval.
* AI cannot approve its own output.
* AI cannot directly modify approved knowledge.
* Ambiguities and assumptions are first-class records.
* Probable contradictions are clearly labeled as probable.
* Clarification questions are structured and traceable.
* Human review actions are audited.
* Only approved candidates may be promoted.
* Promotion creates canonical AEIR versions.
* AI-operation provenance is preserved.
* Usage and cost are recorded.
* Prompt-injection controls are tested.
* Evaluation metrics are automatically produced.
* The unsupported invention rate remains below the accepted threshold.
* R3 snapshot and traceability guarantees remain intact.

---

# 41. Architectural Invariant

The following invariant governs all R4 implementation decisions:

> AI may propose project knowledge, but only validated, traceable, human-approved knowledge may become canonical project truth.

Any feature that allows AI output to bypass candidate staging, deterministic validation, provenance, or human review shall be rejected.

---

# 42. Transition to R5

R5 may begin only after R4 acceptance.

R5 shall introduce the complete clarification and knowledge-convergence workflow:

> Transform unresolved findings, ambiguities, assumptions, and client answers into an approved, internally consistent project knowledge baseline.

R5 shall focus on:

* adaptive clarification cycles
* answer validation
* change proposals
* contradiction resolution
* decision records
* approval gates
* completeness scoring
* knowledge-readiness scoring
* approved baseline creation

R5 shall not yet introduce all five artifact compilers.

The first artifact compiler should begin only after the project knowledge baseline can be declared ready, approved, reproducible, and traceable.

R10-IR-01
AI-Enterprise Verification and Validation Engine Specification

Document ID: R10-IR-01
Version: 1.0.0
Status: Draft
Classification: Constitutional Platform Module
Implementation Target: Future P20
Primary Dependencies: R2–R9
1. Purpose

R10 defines the Verification and Validation Engine (VVE) as the authoritative capability for determining whether an AI-Enterprise implementation:

    was built correctly against its approved contracts; and

    satisfies the approved project requirements and intended outcomes.

R10 receives governed implementation results from R9 and evaluates them against exact requirements, architecture decisions, interfaces, policies, quality attributes, planning gates, and verification obligations.

R10 SHALL produce deterministic, evidence-backed verdicts.

It SHALL distinguish:

Verification
Did the implementation conform to its specified contracts?

Validation
Does the resulting capability satisfy the approved need and intended use?

Successful compilation, passing unit tests, completed tasks, or approved code review SHALL NOT independently prove requirement satisfaction.

R10 establishes the chain:

R9 Verification Handoff
          ↓
Verification scope resolution
          ↓
Environment and evidence validation
          ↓
Static and dynamic execution
          ↓
Contract and policy evaluation
          ↓
Requirement coverage analysis
          ↓
Validation against intended outcomes
          ↓
Independent verdict
          ↓
Evidence publication
          ↓
Requirement satisfaction recommendation

2. Architectural Role

The distinction among R5, R6, R7, R9, R10, and R11 is mandatory.

R5  — Defines requirements and their verification obligations.

R6  — Defines architectural claims and architecture verification obligations.

R7  — Allocates verification work and completion gates.

R9  — Produces an implementation result and verification handoff.

R10 — Executes and governs verification and validation.

R11 — Preserves evidence and audit history.

R10 SHALL evaluate implementation independently from the actor or agent that produced it where policy requires separation of duties.

R10 SHALL NOT:

    approve requirements;

    change architecture;

    modify implementation to make tests pass;

    fabricate missing evidence;

    waive mandatory obligations without governed authority;

    mark a requirement satisfied when required verification remains incomplete.

3. Problem Statement

Software verification commonly becomes unreliable because:

    tests are disconnected from requirements;

    tests validate implementation details rather than obligations;

    generated code is trusted without independent evaluation;

    test environments differ from approved conditions;

    failed checks are ignored;

    flaky tests are retried until they pass;

    coverage percentages conceal missing critical scenarios;

    security and policy checks are performed separately or not at all;

    evidence is incomplete or unauditable;

    validation of actual stakeholder outcomes is omitted.

AI-generated implementation increases these risks.

An AI agent may produce code that:

    passes superficial tests;

    implements the wrong interpretation;

    omits failure behavior;

    violates architecture boundaries;

    weakens authorization;

    uses unsafe dependencies;

    behaves unpredictably outside sample inputs;

    appears correct without satisfying the actual requirement.

R10 provides a governed system for proving implementation conformance and intended fitness.
4. Constitutional Requirements
R10-001 — Exact obligation binding

Every verification execution SHALL bind to exact versions of:

    Requirement Baseline;

    Architecture Baseline;

    Planning Baseline;

    Implementation Slice;

    Change Set;

    repository revision;

    verification obligations;

    applicable policies;

    test assets;

    verification environment.

R10-002 — Independent verdict

Verification verdicts SHALL be calculated from governed results and SHALL NOT be inferred from implementation status.
R10-003 — Complete traceability

Every verification result SHALL trace to:

    one or more requirements;

    acceptance criteria;

    architecture elements or decisions where applicable;

    implementation outputs;

    executed procedures;

    produced evidence.

R10-004 — No evidence, no pass

A verification obligation SHALL NOT receive a PASSED verdict without valid required evidence.
R10-005 — No silent omission

Unexecuted, skipped, blocked, unavailable, or waived checks SHALL be explicitly represented.
R10-006 — Criticality-based rigor

Verification depth SHALL be proportional to requirement criticality, risk, policy, and architecture impact.
R10-007 — Environment integrity

Verification results SHALL identify and validate the environment in which they were obtained.
R10-008 — Reproducibility

Verification SHALL be reproducible from recorded inputs, procedures, environments, tools, versions, and data where technically possible.
R10-009 — Separation of verification and validation

The engine SHALL report verification and validation verdicts separately.
R10-010 — Governed waiver

A mandatory verification obligation MAY be waived only by authorized governance and SHALL include justification, risk acceptance, scope, duration, and compensating controls.
R10-011 — Failure preservation

Failed results SHALL remain immutable and auditable, even after later successful execution.
R10-012 — AI authority boundary

AI may generate test candidates, analyze results, classify failures, and recommend corrective actions.

AI SHALL NOT alter raw results, approve its own critical verification, fabricate evidence, or convert an incomplete outcome into a pass.

31. Retry Policy

Retries SHALL be permitted only when:

    the failure classification is retryable;

    inputs and environment state are known;

    policy permits retry;

    the prior result remains preserved;

    the attempt number increments;

    retry does not conceal a deterministic defect.

A retry after implementation change SHALL belong to a new repository revision or verification baseline.

50. Failure Modes and Recovery
50.1 Environment qualification failure

Execution SHALL not start. The environment SHALL be corrected or replaced.
50.2 Tool failure

The execution SHALL be classified as ERROR, not FAIL, unless the obligation explicitly defines tool failure as failure.
50.3 Test asset corruption

The asset SHALL be rejected through integrity validation.
50.4 Evidence-store outage

Authoritative pass verdicts requiring durable evidence SHALL fail closed.
50.5 Policy-service outage

New critical policy-dependent verification SHALL fail closed.
50.6 Partial campaign execution

Completed results SHALL remain valid only if environment and input integrity remain valid. Remaining obligations SHALL be explicit.
50.7 Repository revision changes

The current Verification Baseline SHALL be invalidated for affected procedures.
50.8 Non-reproducible result

The result SHALL be marked for investigation and may become inconclusive or flaky.
50.9 Validation authority unavailable

Technical verification MAY complete, but final validation SHALL remain incomplete.
50.10 Result integrity mismatch

The result SHALL be quarantined and excluded from positive verdict calculation.
